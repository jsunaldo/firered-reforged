The idea

Same game. Better game.

FireRed Legacy follows the philosophy of TheSmithPlays' Legacy trilogy: keep the Gen 3 soul — no physical/special split, no new types, no fairy — and fix everything else. Kanto's trainers, wild Pokémon, and key items follow Pokémon Yellow Legacy. Every stat, moveset, learnset, and evolution follows Pokémon Emerald Legacy. The result is the definitive way to play Kanto on the GBA engine.

The rival

He got an Eevee.
You got a problem.

In Oak's Lab, your rival reaches for a starter — and Oak stops him. “Trade it here. I've been saving something special for you… EEVEE!” His Eevee evolves into whichever Eeveelution counters your starter, and his teams follow Yellow Legacy's rival battles move for move.

Pick Squirtle

He raises a Jolteon. Your Water types are never safe.

Pick Bulbasaur

He raises a Flareon. Bring a fire extinguisher.

Pick Charmander

He raises a Vaporeon. Hope you packed Thunderbolt.

And after you take his title? He benches the Eeveelution — “The POKéMON GRAMPS gave me is taking a well-earned rest… I rebuilt this team myself. No handouts, no crutch!” — and returns with Alakazam, Machamp, Tyranitar, Gyarados, Salamence, and Arcanine at Lv 75–77. Six types. Full coverage. No free wins.

The main game

Kanto, as Yellow Legacy drew it.

Every route trainer, gym leader, and Elite Four member runs their Yellow Legacy team, translated to Gen 3. Every wild encounter table follows Yellow Legacy's map design. Every boss holds items by Emerald Legacy convention — Sitrus Berries on aces, Leftovers on stallers.

Trainers with intent

300+ route trainer teams rebuilt from Yellow Legacy. Gym leaders and the Elite Four keep their famous YL sets — Fissure Onix, Horn Drill Omastar, the works. Boss Pokémon carry battle items and real IVs.

Wild Kanto, redesigned

All 60 Kanto maps use Yellow Legacy's encounter tables — including the full Old/Good/Super Rod fishing system. Pikachu is still in Viridian Forest, where it belongs.

Trade evolutions, retired

Kadabra and Haunter evolve at Lv 42, Machoke and Graveler at 38. Metal Coat, King's Rock, Dragon Scale, Up-Grade, and the Deep Sea items are used straight from the bag, like stones.

Both fossils. One save.

Pick your fossil in Mt. Moon as always — then visit the Fuchsia fishing guru, who "fished up" the other one in the Seafoam Islands and hands it over at Lv 10.

Cross-gen evolutions, day one

Crobat, Steelix, Scizor, Slowking, Politoed, Porygon2, Blissey, Espeon, Umbreon and the rest of Kanto's extended family are obtainable in the main game.

A level curve that means it

Trainer levels climb smoothly from Viridian Forest to a Lv 55–65 Elite Four. Verified segment by segment against the exp actually available — challenging, never grindy.

Any order, always a fight

Like Yellow Legacy, Erika, Koga, Sabrina and Blaine can be challenged in any order — and each fields a team scaled to your badge count (Lv ~35 up to ~53). No more trivial gyms for taking the scenic route.

Raise the road not taken

A FireRed Legacy twist: Route 21 North's grass now hides Bulbasaur, Squirtle, Charmander and Eevee (Mr. Mime still lurks). Surf down from Pallet and raise the starter line you passed on.

Meet the ace

Every gym leader, Elite Four member, and the Champion now stands beside their signature Pokémon in the overworld — Onix looming by Brock, Gengar by Agatha, Dragonite by Lance. You see who you're up against before the ball drops.

Your partner walks with you

HeartGold/SoulSilver-style following Pokémon: your lead marches a step behind you across all of Kanto and the Sevii Islands, with a unique overworld sprite for every one of the 386 species. It ducks away for surfing, cycling and the Poké Center nurse, then rejoins on your very next step.

Koga's daughter guards the gym

A Yellow Legacy tribute: Janine now stands at her father's side in the Fuchsia Gym — an optional ninja duel with Golbat, Ariados and Venomoth before you ever reach Koga.

Battle engine

Gen 3, with the sharp edges filed off.

Emerald Legacy's engine changes, ported line for line.

Ghost is special. Dark is physical.

The one category swap the era begged for. Gengar's Shadow Ball finally runs off Sp. Atk. Tyranitar's Crunch finally runs off Attack. Type IDs are untouched, so trades stay compatible.

Weather that matters

Hail grants Ice-types 1.5× Defense. Sandstorm grants Rock-types — and Sand Veil Pokémon — 1.5× Sp. Def. Blizzard never misses in hail.

132 moves rebalanced

Emerald Legacy's full Gen 3 move pass, applied verbatim — powers, accuracies, PP, and effects. Every change is listed in the ChangeDoc, before → after.

Abilities with teeth

Magma Armor shrugs off Water moves (Sp. Atk ÷8) with its own battle message. Leaf Blade and Sky Attack crit high. Species stats and abilities follow Emerald Legacy's rebalance.

Postgame

Beat the Champion.
Unlock two more generations.

The Sevii Islands 4–7 become a true Johto & Hoenn region — wilds, trainers, and legends. Islands 1–3 stay Kanto, so the main game's rules stay pure.

A wild frontier

Every remaining Johto and Hoenn species roams islands 4–7 — themed by island, with guaranteed-coverage dungeons so the full 386 is provably obtainable (evolution and breeding included). Babies come via Sea and Lax Incense, sold with every evolution item on Two Island.

71 trainers worth fighting

Every island 4–7 trainer runs a class-themed Johto/Hoenn team at Lv 52–58. Rocket Warehouse grunts with Houndoom and Sneasel. Ruin Maniacs with Claydol and Relicanth. Twins with Plusle and Minun. Every Painter has a Smeargle. Obviously.

13 legendary encounters

Raikou, Entei, and Suicune prowl the islands. The Regis wait in the Tanoby chambers. Kyogre, Groudon, and Rayquaza anchor the deep dungeons. Latias, Latios, Celebi, and Jirachi round it out — all with real overworld sprites, all as walk-up encounters. The event tickets bring Ho-Oh, Lugia, and Deoxys.

The Mew quest

Complete the Kanto 150 and Professor Oak asks to see you — then challenges you himself, legendary birds at Lv 77–81. Win, and Mew waits in the Pokémon Mansion basement at Lv 70. It obeys. No truck required.

Two secret champions

A Yellow Legacy tribute hidden until you enter the Hall of Fame: Nurse Joy in the Fuchsia Pokémon Center and Officer Jenny on the streets of Vermilion each raise a full Lv 65 team — a healer's bruisers and a cop's pursuit squad — battle sprites ported and recolored just for them.

The Johto Isles

Sixteen leaders. Eight islands.
One ferry west.

Once your Pokédex spans every region, a captain at One Island Harbor sails you to a chain of eight new islands — each with wild Pokémon themed to a type, and two Gym Leaders to topple: a Johto leader and a same-level Hoenn leader. Beat both and the ferry carries you onward.

Winona guards Skyreach beside Falkner. Wattson sparks up Chitin with Bugsy. Norman blocks Meadowmoo with Whitney. Tate & Liza haunt Hollow with Morty. Brawly trades blows on Ironfist with Chuck. Roxanne fortifies Foundry with Jasmine. Wallace floods Glacier beside Pryce. And Flannery brings the fire to Clair's Wyrmspire.

Quality of life

Modern hands. Nostalgic heart.

IV & EV viewer

On the summary Skills page, press A: live stats → IVs → EVs. Stat values are nature-colored — red boosted, blue hindered. The judge is built in.

Repel, again?

When a Repel wears off, the game offers another of the same kind. Yes with one press.

Instant text

The FAST setting now prints whole messages in a single frame. SLOW and MID untouched for the purists.

Run everywhere

Running Shoes work indoors. It was time.

League TM shop

Indigo Plateau sells Ice Beam, Thunderbolt, Flamethrower, Giga Drain, SolarBeam, Brick Break, and Rock Tomb — Yellow Legacy's league shop, in Gen 3 money.

No dead quests

The Exp. Share aide gives freely — no 50-caught gate. Custom events carry Quest Log guards, so save-replays can't corrupt them.

Forgettable HMs, reusable TMs

HMs come off a move slot whenever you like, and TMs are never used up. No more HM slaves or one-shot TMs — the Legacy standard.

Cut trees, cut for good

A tree you cut never grows back. Fuchsia's pointless cut trees are gone, and its PC/Gym now open from the right side too — no more lap around the top.

Every evolution item

The Celadon Dept. Store counter stocks all of them — the four stones, Sun/Moon, and the trade-evo items you now use straight from the bag.

Find a Pokémon

The Compendium's search box tells you every wild spot a Pokémon appears — method, odds, and levels — the moment you type its name.

For challenge runners

Built with Nuzlockes in mind.

Some trainers live by hard rules. One Pokémon per route. Nicknames for every friend. A fainted partner sleeps forever. FireRed Legacy respects the run.

The Supply Gentleman

A distinguished fellow by Pallet Town's south fence hands out Rare Candies, Master Balls, Full Restores, Max Repels, and Max Elixirs — 99 at a time, as often as you like. Spend your evening on the run, not the grind. (Hard mode's level caps still apply to candies.)

The Route Planner

An included reference lists all 132 areas in game order with every possible first encounter, combined odds per method, and level ranges — generated from the game's real data, not a wiki.

Fish from minute one

The Old Rod moved to Viridian City's Mart, Yellow Legacy style — your early-route encounter options open up before the first badge.
